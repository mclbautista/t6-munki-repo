## Why this matters

Ticket 13's criterion 4 requires the evidence that this restriction is what stands between the
repository and the wider network, "including that the repository must never be internet-reachable".
The finding that reshaped ticket 13 was that **no firewall is installed on the NAS at all**
(`appcenter_list_apps` shows no QuFirewall or equivalent). Whether a cloud relay or a DDNS name gives
the NAS a path from the internet is the other half of that question, so 13D asked for it to be
verified rather than assumed.

Read together: nothing on the NAS side creates an internet path. A myQNAPcloud relay needs a linked
QNAP ID; there is none. A published name needs a published service; there is none. A DDNS name needs
an enabled provider; there is none. A router forward opened by the NAS itself needs Auto Router
Configuration; it is force-disabled with QTS's own reason code. The app being installed and enabled
means the *capability* is present and could be turned on from the UI — it is not a path by itself.

The HBS finding is a different fact that matters to ticket 12B: the off-NAS copy has **no cloud
destination configured today**, which is consistent with the approved recommendation (an HBS job to
another device, plus the metadata's Git history pushed off the NAS) rather than a cloud service.

## What is NOT established, and why

- **Reachability from the public internet was not verified by us.** The intended off-network probe
  (fetching the NAS's external address `27.49.72.161` on ports 80/443/8082 from a host outside T6's
  network) **could not be made**: the browser backend refuses to fetch a bare IP address
  (`Blocked: URL targets a private or internal address`), and that guard was not worked around. No
  off-network vantage point is available to this session.
- **The gateway is the remaining unknown.** Whether a *manual* port-forward to the NAS exists on the
  router (the Deco mesh or the Unifi switch/router), and whether the ISP address is public or
  carrier-shared, are facts only the gateway UI can show. The NAS-side evidence above says the NAS
  itself did not create such a forward; it cannot speak for a hand-made one.
- Per decision **13C**, the negative case for ticket 13 is recorded as **not verified by us**, with
  exactly what remains unverified stated — the reachability of the metadata endpoint from an address
  outside the fleet's path, which will be a routing fact rather than a firewall refusal because 13A
  chose network segregation over a firewall.
